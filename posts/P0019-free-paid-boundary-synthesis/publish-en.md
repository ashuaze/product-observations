### 1/6

1/6 A free tier is a classifier: it chooses what to subsidize, what signals “ready to pay,” and what happens at the boundary.

Across six cases, four questions matter: what is metered, when does it appear, who bears failure, and what evidence would change the rule?

### 2/6

2/6 Meter behavior that predicts cost or value.

Calendly gates templates. Notion changes block limits when a second member joins. Vercel gates commercial intent.

Each chooses a different upgrade candidate. A countable signal may still miss the job.

https://calendly.com/pricing

### 3/6

3/6 Free can fund ecosystem value. Standard GitHub-hosted runners are free for public repos; private repos get quotas.

Publicness may reward contribution; it cannot measure what a private team can pay. Subsidy and segmentation need separate evidence.

https://docs.github.com/en/billing/concepts/product-billing/github-actions

### 4/6

4/6 Locate the failure. Zoom Basic ends almost all meetings at 40 minutes; free Google Meet allows 1:1 calls up to 24 hours, but groups only 60.

The host controls entitlement; everyone bears interruption. Shared work needs warning and a fallback.

https://support.google.com/meet/answer/7317473?hl=en

### 5/6

5/6 Shared quotas expand failure. Full iCloud storage can stop backups, photo uploads, iCloud Drive sync and iCloud Mail. Full Google storage blocks Drive, Photos and Gmail.

If one pool serves several jobs, show which task fails next—not only “90% used.”

https://support.apple.com/en-us/108922

### 6/6

6/6 Before shipping a free limit, finish this:

We meter [X] because it predicts [cost/value].
[Actor] can act before [task] fails.
Users can [safe fallback].
We track conversion with completion, workarounds, support, retention and unit cost—and revise if [evidence].

### 1/5

1/5 Deleting an account is not one state change. X and LinkedIn split the exit differently—and expose a design question: should “I want to disappear” mean hide me, start a deletion clock, or erase everything now?

### 2/5

2/5 On X, deactivation starts a 30-day path to deletion. Return within that window and you can reactivate; do nothing and the account is deleted. That protects against impulsive exits, but one state must serve both “take a break” and “leave.”

https://help.x.com/en/managing-your-account/how-to-deactivate-x-account

### 3/5

3/5 The surprising failure mode is the reactivation path. X warns that logging in reactivates an account, so connected third-party apps should be revoked first. A recovery door is also an entry point: an integration can undermine an exit.

https://help.x.com/en/managing-your-account/connect-or-revoke-access-to-third-party-apps

### 4/5

4/5 LinkedIn splits the intents. Hibernate: hide the profile; keep posts/comments. Close: reopen in most cases within 14 days, but endorsements, recommendations, pending invitations, followings and group memberships won’t return.

https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1336653

https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a1379064

### 5/5

5/5 The PM lesson isn’t “longer undo is better.” Specify an exit contract: what disappears now; what is retained and until when; what can reactivate it; what returns; and what may persist outside the product. A countdown alone is only partial reversibility.

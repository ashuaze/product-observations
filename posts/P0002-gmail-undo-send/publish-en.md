### 1/6

1/6
Gmail gives you up to 30 seconds to reconsider a send.

The useful design question is where to put that second chance: before sending, just after the click, or after delivery. Each offers a different opportunity to catch a mistake.

### 2/6

2/6
On the web, Gmail's Undo Send window is 5, 10, 20 or 30 seconds.

This is a brief opportunity to cancel sending, not a way to erase an email someone has already received or read.

https://support.google.com/mail/answer/2819488?hl=en

### 3/6

3/6
Gmail on Android also offers confirmation before sending.

My read: confirmation adds a decision to each send; a delay makes even correct messages wait. Their value depends on when users notice the error.

https://support.google.com/mail/answer/6562?co=GENIE.Platform%3DAndroid&hl=en

### 4/6

4/6
Post-delivery recall is a third option. Exchange Online can delete eligible delivered messages, subject to policy and recipient-organization permissions.

Removing the message cannot undo what a reader learned.

https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/exchange/mail-flow-best-practices/work-with-cloud-based-message-recall

### 5/6

5/6
My decision rule: use a short delay when mistakes surface quickly and waiting is cheap. For sensitive messages, also check recipients and attachments before release.

If the error is only noticed tomorrow, a 30-second window cannot help.

### 6/6

6/6
To choose a delay, I'd measure when errors are noticed, how many harmful sends are prevented, and whether waiting disrupts the task.

More Undo clicks alone would not prove success: users may be making harmless edits or cancelling correct messages.

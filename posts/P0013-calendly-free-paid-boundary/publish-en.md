### 1/6

1/6 A busy Calendly user can stay free; a quieter one can hit a paid boundary.

Free allows unlimited one-on-one meetings, but only one active event type. Standard unlocks unlimited types.

The limit is on reusable setups, not bookings.

https://calendly.com/pricing

### 2/6

2/6 An event type is a reusable meeting template.

For illustration: one template used 50 times fits the limit. Two templates available at once do not, even if each gets only two bookings.

A booking cap would split those users differently.

### 3/6

3/6 Important exception: Free also includes One-off Meetings with custom duration and location. The link expires after booking; moving the meeting requires canceling and creating another.

Occasional exceptions need not mean upgrading.

https://calendly.com/help/how-to-create-a-one-off-meeting

### 4/6

4/6 The counterexample is Cal.com: its free individual plan includes unlimited event types and calendars. Paid Teams adds shared availability and round-robin scheduling.

Same scheduling job; a different paid boundary.

https://cal.com/pricing

### 5/6

5/6 My reading: a template limit can charge solo users for avoiding repeated setup. Leaving templates free can serve complex solo workflows without requiring conversion.

Neither proves a better business model. Both products bundle other paid benefits.

### 6/6

6/6 Before copying this limit, test whether repeated setup work predicts willingness to pay better than booking count.

Occasional exceptions may not justify more templates. And if each booking is costly to serve, a volume cap may fit better.

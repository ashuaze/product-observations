### 1/4

1/4
Read receipts look like status metadata. They also create a reply deadline the product never states.

Once “read” is visible, opening a message can feel like accepting a task. People may delay opening just to keep the option to answer later.

### 2/4

2/4
Signal makes read receipts reciprocal: both people must enable them. iMessage lets each recipient choose whether to send them, globally or per contact.

Same signal, different contract: mutual exchange versus one-sided disclosure.

### 3/4

3/4
Evidence is mixed, not universal. Survey and interview studies link visible reads to speculation, strategic delay, and pressure. But a 2025 U.S. study found many users preferred reciprocity because it felt fair and simpler.

### 4/4

4/4
Match status to the job:
- urgent: “seen”
- async: “acknowledged” or “reply later”
- casual: private reading

Measure delayed opening and opt-outs, not only reply speed. If people postpone reading to avoid the signal, the design moved friction.

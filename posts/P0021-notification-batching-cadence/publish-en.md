### 1/5

1/5
Notifications are habit cues: each arrival can pull a user back. The usual control is binary—on or off. But cadence may matter more than count.

The useful question: which events deserve interruption, and which can wait for a review moment?

### 2/5

2/5
iPhone's Scheduled Summary lets users choose apps and delivery times. Each app can use Immediate Delivery or join the summary; Time Sensitive notifications remain immediate.

This is a three-lane model: now, later, or off.

https://support.apple.com/guide/iphone/view-and-respond-to-notifications-iph6534c01bc/ios

### 3/5

3/5
A 2019 randomized field experiment assigned 237 Android users in India to normal alerts, hourly batches, 3 daily batches, or no alerts for 2 weeks.

Three batches reduced unlocks and improved reported concentration, control and productivity.

https://doi.org/10.1016/j.chb.2019.07.016

### 4/5

4/5
The shape mattered. Hourly batching was mostly indistinguishable from normal delivery. No alerts reduced interruptions but raised notification-related FoMO and anxiety, without improving attention.

Less interruption was not automatically better.

### 5/5

5/5
Design notifications by urgency and rhythm:
• Immediate: action loses value if delayed.
• Batched: awareness matters; timing can wait.
• Off: low value even in review.

Measure interruptions, self-checks, missed urgent events and opt-outs—not opens alone.

### 1/6

1/6
Autoplay changes the default decision from "start another episode" to "stop the next one." That can remove useful friction—or let a session outlive the user's intent.

The real design question is when a product should ask again.

### 2/6

2/6
YouTube previews the next video and lets users cancel or disable Autoplay. It also asks "Continue watching?" during uninterrupted playback: within 30 min on mobile, at 60 min on web, or 180 min on TV.

https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12819304?hl=en

### 3/6

3/6
Netflix lets each profile toggle next-episode autoplay, then interrupts passive continuation. On TVs and streaming devices, "Are you still watching?" appears after 3 episodes and 90 minutes of uninterrupted viewing.

https://help.netflix.com/en/node/114059

### 4/6

4/6
A 2025 randomized study of 76 US Netflix users found that disabling autoplay reduced logged playback by 21 min/day and average session length by 17.6 min during a 10–17 day study. It found no significant change in time between sessions.

https://doi.org/10.1145/3710928

### 5/6

5/6
The counterexample matters: about half of the treatment group planned to turn autoplay back on, citing convenience. A checkpoint can also interrupt deliberate TV, music or hands-busy use.

The right boundary depends on how stable intent is.

### 6/6

6/6
Design for intent decay:
• Make continuation visible and reversible.
• Use context: idle controls, episode count, time, device.
• Let users set the cadence.
• Measure early exits after autoplay, prompt outcomes and regret—not watch time alone.

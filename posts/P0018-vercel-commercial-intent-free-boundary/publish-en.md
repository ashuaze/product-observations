### 1/6

1/6 Most free tiers ask: how much did you use? Vercel Hobby also asks: why does the deployment exist?

Hobby is limited to personal, non-commercial use. Any commercial deployment requires Pro or Enterprise—even below Hobby’s resource caps.

https://vercel.com/docs/limits/fair-use-guidelines

### 2/6

2/6 Vercel defines commercial use broadly: taking payments, running ads, promoting sales, affiliate-first sites, or anyone being paid to create, update or host the project.

One exception: asking for donations does not count as commercial use.

### 3/6

3/6 This creates a revealing pair. A personal, volunteer-built donation page passes the commercial-use test. A tiny brochure built by a paid freelancer does not—even before it makes a sale.

Similar infrastructure load; different eligibility.

### 4/6

4/6 Purpose is not the only meter. Hobby also has resource caps; after most are exceeded, the feature pauses until 30 days have passed. Pro includes more resources and allows pay-as-you-go usage.

Vercel gates eligibility first, then consumption.

https://vercel.com/docs/plans/hobby

### 5/6

5/6 Cloudflare Workers offers a different published meter: Free includes 100,000 requests/day and 10 ms of CPU time per invocation. Pages Functions share that request quota.

It is easier to audit, but value and usage can still diverge.

https://developers.cloudflare.com/workers/platform/pricing/

### 6/6

6/6 Choose the meter by the failure you can afford.

Purpose gate: captures value early, but creates ambiguity.
Usage gate: tracks cost, but misses ability to pay.
Hybrid: covers both, but needs examples and migration.

Measure disputes, upgrades, project survival and cost.

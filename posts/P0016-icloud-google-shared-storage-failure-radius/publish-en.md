### 1/5

1/5 A shared storage quota does more than pool capacity. It couples failure across products.

A photo can consume the last free byte. The next visible failure may be a device backup, a file sync—or an incoming email.

### 2/5

2/5 Apple gives 5 GB of free iCloud storage across backups, Photos, Drive, Mail and more. When it is full, device backups stop, new photos stop uploading, iCloud Drive stops staying current, and iCloud Mail cannot send or receive.

https://support.apple.com/en-us/108922

### 3/5

3/5 Google Accounts include up to 15 GB shared across Gmail, Drive and Photos. At the limit, users cannot create or upload Drive files, back up Photos, or send and receive Gmail; incoming messages can bounce to the sender.

https://support.google.com/drive/answer/6374270?hl=en

### 4/5

4/5 The counterpoint: pooled quotas let users allocate capacity to the service they value. Separate caps can strand unused space.

So the choice is not “shared is bad.” It is flexibility versus a larger failure radius.

### 5/5

5/5 Design rule: if one pool can disable several jobs, warn about consequences—not just “90% used.”

Show which action is growing the pool and which tasks will fail next. Measure warning-driven cleanup, blocked tasks and recovery time—not only upgrades.

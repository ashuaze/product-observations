### 1/5

1/5 Say you play the same piano track every night to fall asleep. The next morning, Spotify might recommend more piano. The plays are real. The leap is assuming you want more piano when you’re looking for something new.

### 2/5

2/5 Exclude your sleep playlist from Spotify’s taste profile and past and future plays from that playlist have less influence on recommendations. Play the exact same track from Liked Songs and that listen still counts.

https://support.spotify.com/us/article/exclude-playlists-or-tracks-from-your-taste-profile/

### 3/5

3/5 If one track keeps waking you up, “Hide in this playlist” stops it from playing there. If the playlist works perfectly but keeps steering your recommendations toward sleep music, excluding the playlist lets you keep listening.

### 4/5

4/5 A playlist works as a shortcut for intent when it serves one job. A playlist for both sleep and finding new favorites is harder to handle. Excluding it gives less weight to the discoveries too. Use the smallest scope people can recognize and manage.

### 5/5

5/5 Test whether people can predict what happens when they play the same track elsewhere. Then check whether they get fewer unwanted recommendations after the next refresh while still finding music they want to save. Count both before calling it a win.

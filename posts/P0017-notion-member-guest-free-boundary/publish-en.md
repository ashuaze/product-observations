### 1/4

1/4 Notion’s Free plan has an unusual switch: a one-person workspace gets unlimited blocks. Add a second member, and the workspace gets a 1,000-block limit.

The pricing boundary changes when collaboration changes—even if the content doesn’t.

https://www.notion.com/help/understanding-block-usage

### 2/4

2/4 This is a creation counter, not a storage quota. Notion counts blocks created since signup; deleting blocks or emptying Trash does not reduce it.

At the limit, existing content stays editable. A three-day grace period gives the team time to decide.

### 3/4

3/4 Guests reveal the alternative. Free allows up to 10 external guests, but each guest only sees invited pages and subpages—not the whole workspace. Paid plans charge per member.

My reading: the upgrade partly prices breadth of shared context.

https://www.notion.com/help/whos-who-in-a-workspace

### 4/4

4/4 Before copying seat-based pricing, ask what the seat unlocks.

Test guest vs member work: sharing steps, search time, role mistakes and upgrades. If page-level access completes the job, the boundary may be early. If it creates coordination costs, broader access has value.

### 1/6

1/6
iPhone's Offload App separates two things we often bundle together: the software and the user's accumulated work.

That creates a useful option for a full phone. But a reversible choice is only as good as the path back.

### 2/6

2/6
Apple's distinction:

Offload App removes the app but keeps its documents and data.
Delete App removes the app and its related local data.

One frees space while preserving work; the other clears that local state.

https://support.apple.com/en-us/108429

### 3/6

3/6
The payoff depends on what takes up space. If documents dominate, offloading keeps most of the problem.

I'd offer it when the app itself is large, saved work matters, and returning can wait.

### 4/6

4/6
Returning requires a download. Apple says you can reinstall an app if it's still available.

So "rarely used" is not enough reason to offload an app needed offline. Preserving data doesn't guarantee access when it matters.

https://support.apple.com/en-au/guide/iphone/iph248b543ca/27/ios/27

### 5/6

5/6
Apple says deleting an app doesn't delete its data stored in iCloud. It also doesn't automatically cancel in-app subscriptions.

Local removal, account deletion and ending billing need distinct explanations.

https://support.apple.com/en-us/101550

### 6/6

6/6
For builders: judge reversibility by whether users can resume the task, how long it takes, and what they lose.

Offer a temporary exit when saved work matters and return is dependable. Keep deletion and subscription cancellation easy to find.

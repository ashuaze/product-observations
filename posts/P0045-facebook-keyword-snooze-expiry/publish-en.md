### 1/3

1/3 In 2018, Facebook tested Keyword Snooze: hide Feed posts containing a chosen word or phrase for 30 days. Spikes in use in a small early sample prompted a seven-day test. That raised a design question: when should a rejection expire?

https://about.fb.com/news/2018/08/inside-feed-keyword-snooze-people-turn-down-the-noise/

### 2/3

2/3 A 2021 update says the test had ended. Neither team report gives a 7-vs-30-day result. Short pauses may require renewals; long ones may hide content after the need passes. A spike in new snoozes doesn't show when those users wanted content back.

https://about.fb.com/news/2018/06/keyword-snooze-a-new-way-to-help-control-your-news-feed/

### 3/3

3/3 My design takeaway: offer temporary pauses for temporary needs, with a clear expiry and a lasting option for enduring dislikes. Compare unwanted returns and useful rediscoveries after expiry. Snooze uptake alone can't settle how long a pause should last.

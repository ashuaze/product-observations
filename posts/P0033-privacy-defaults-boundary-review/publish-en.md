### 1/6

1/6 Turn off location sharing in a Google Photos album, then download and email the photo. The file can still carry the location your camera saved.

A privacy review should follow the data through four handoffs. https://support.google.com/photos/answer/6153599?hl=en

### 2/6

2/6 Collection: Android 12 and later let users grant approximate location even when an app asks for precise. Try a city-level search with approximate access first. Ask for more when a task, such as turn-by-turn navigation, actually needs it.

### 3/6

3/6 Audience: Strava can hide the ends of a route while leaving the activity public. An unlisted YouTube video can reach anyone who gets the link. Check who can open the item and what they can see once it's open.

### 4/6

4/6 Timing: On desktop Chrome, one Incognito window left open keeps the session going. In Signal, a disappearing message's timer starts at send for the sender and at read for the recipient. Write the trigger next to the duration.

### 5/6

5/6 A Followers-only Strava activity won't appear on public segment leaderboards. For a fitness app, try keeping routine logs private and asking for wider visibility when someone chooses to compete.

### 6/6

6/6 Before shipping, ask someone to predict who can see what. Repeat the task, changing one thing: the permission, the viewer, the clock trigger, or the sharing route. Check what actually arrives or remains, and whether they can still finish the task.

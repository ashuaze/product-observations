### 1/6

1/6
Duolingo lets you miss a day without losing your streak.

The interesting design question is how much room for error helps people return.

Its team tested one, two and three Streak Freezes. More wasn't always better.

### 2/6

2/6
A Streak Freeze must be equipped before a missed day. It preserves the streak across that gap.

My read: this gives a missed day a different meaning. The record survives, leaving the next session connected to earlier effort.

### 3/6

3/6
Duolingo's account of an earlier test reports a 0.38% relative rise in daily active learners after allowing two freezes.

This measures activity; it does not establish better language proficiency.

https://blog.duolingo.com/how-duolingo-streak-builds-habit/

### 4/6

4/6
In a 2024 interview, Duolingo's Jackson Shuttleworth said three freezes performed no better than two.

His explanation: more chances to return with an intact streak competed with longer absences.

https://www.linkedin.com/posts/lennyrachitsky_the-thinking-behind-duolingos-streak-freezes-activity-7275197877256179713-tvBJ

### 5/6

5/6
I'd evaluate a freeze policy on three separate outcomes:

- Returning after a missed day
- Actual practice days over the next month
- Learning progress

A longer streak can coexist with fewer practice days. The counter alone cannot tell you whether the policy works.

### 6/6

6/6
My takeaway: where an occasional missed session is recoverable, test a limited allowance for missed days.

Set its size by whether users resume meaningful practice. The right amount of flexibility depends on the activity and the cost of an interruption.

### 1/6

1/6
Slack's free plan gives one message two clocks:

- after 90 days, it can disappear from view
- after one year, it may be permanently deleted

That distinction matters: limiting access to memory is different from destroying it.

### 2/6

2/6
Slack says free workspaces can view and search the last 90 days. Owners choose whether to keep hidden data for up to one year or delete it after 90 days.

Those are not equivalent free plans.

https://slack.com/help/articles/115002422943-Usage-limits-for-free-workspaces

### 3/6

3/6
Hidden is restricted access; deleted is permanent loss.

Slack's pricing page says upgrading can unlock the previous 12 months. But that only helps if the workspace retained the data. With 90-day deletion, there is nothing older to unlock.

https://slack.com/pricing

### 4/6

4/6
A pin or save is not an archive. Slack says retention rules also apply to pinned and saved items.

So the failure mode is subtle: a team marks a decision as important, but the storage policy can still remove it.

https://slack.com/help/articles/203457187-Customize-data-retention-in-Slack

### 5/6

5/6
My read: Slack is not only separating free and paid storage. It is pricing the point at which chat becomes institutional memory.

That can be a reasonable value boundary. It becomes risky when teams mistake "not visible" for "safely archived."

### 6/6

6/6
A decision rule: if chat is disposable coordination, 90 days may be enough. If it is the record of decisions, retention is core infrastructure.

Products should make three states explicit: visible, retained, deleted. An upgrade cannot recover data already gone.

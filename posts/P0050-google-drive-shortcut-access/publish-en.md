### 1/3

1/3 A Google Drive shortcut can sit in a shared folder while some teammates still can't open its target. Creating the shortcut doesn't automatically change the original file's permissions. Adding a route and granting access are separate changes.

https://support.google.com/drive/answer/9700156?hl=en&co=GENIE.Platform%3DDesktop

### 2/3

2/3 A shortcut links to the original; it doesn't preserve a separate copy of its contents. People who can access its folder or drive can see the shortcut's title, even if they can't open the original. Keeping content restricted can still expose the title.

### 3/3

3/3 Drive may prompt you to grant target access when adding a shortcut. Treat that as a distinct permission choice. For shared collections, confirm both that the reference was added and that the intended readers can open the original.

### 1/5

1/5 A like count serves two users at once: feedback for the creator, and social proof for the viewer. Making it public can motivate posting—and turn every post into a visible comparison.

“Hide likes” is not one decision. It changes who can compare whom.

### 2/5

2/5 Instagram separates two controls: hide counts on posts in your feed; or hide public counts on your own post, before or after publishing.

The first changes what shapes your judgment. The second changes what others can use to judge you.

https://about.fb.com/news/2021/05/giving-people-more-control/

### 3/5

3/5 Meta said hidden counts were useful for some and annoying to others, partly because counts signal what is popular.

That is the countercase: reducing comparison can also remove information. Meta’s summary is company-reported, not independent effect evidence.

### 4/5

4/5 A 3-week randomized field experiment hid likes, follower totals and view counts for 123 Instagram users. They viewed fewer photos, but average positive affect and flourishing did not improve.

Effects varied by Instagram-context neuroticism.

https://doi.org/10.21203/rs.3.rs-10225039/v1

### 5/5

5/5 Design metrics by audience:
• Private feedback helps creators learn.
• Public counts add social proof—and comparison.
• Hidden counts make content stand alone.

Test posting, useful replies, passive viewing and comparison pressure. Likes are a signal, not the outcome.
